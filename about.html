About

I'm an M.Tech student in the Integrated Design & Innovation (iDD) program at IIT Madras, currently working on a thesis under Prof. Pulkit Verma: Flat-Foldable Origami Tessellations as Native Continuous Toolpaths for Fused Deposition Modeling. The central idea is that computational origami — a mathematically rich theory of how flat sheets fold — is also a source of practical structure for problems in manufacturing, generative design, and robotics that would otherwise require solving hard combinatorial or geometric problems from scratch.

That thread runs through the projects on this site: using Maekawa's theorem to get crossing-free 3D-printer toolpaths for free instead of forcing them with mesh refinement; building a diffusion model that can generate novel origami patterns while staying on the vanishingly small manifold of patterns that actually fold flat; and inverse-designing origami-style linkages that convert a single muscle contraction into complex, multi-directional motion for biohybrid robots.

I like problems where a clean piece of mathematics — a graph-theoretic theorem, a closure constraint, a symmetry — turns out to do real engineering work: making something fabricable, learnable, or controllable that would otherwise be intractable by brute force.
